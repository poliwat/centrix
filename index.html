The founding argument

Three things have gotten worse for ~100 years — no matter who was in chargeLeft and right argue fiercely over social questions. Meanwhile, the measures that actually decide whether ordinary Americans thrive have declined across administrations of every party. This is the problem Centrix exists to solve.

Decline No. 1 — A roof of your own
3× → 5×

Housing left working families behind

A typical new home cost about 3× median income around 1970. By 2024 the median home price reached roughly 5× income — and a median-earning household would spend ~41.8% of income on the median home, far above the 30% affordability line.

Sources: Visual Capitalist; Harvard JCHS; Zoocasa.

Decline No. 2 — The war on the middle class
90% vs 33%

Pay stopped tracking the work

From 1979–2025, worker productivity grew about 90% while typical worker pay grew just 33% — productivity rose roughly 3.5× as fast as pay. Had pay kept pace, the typical worker would earn about $16/hour more today.

Source: Economic Policy Institute (Productivity–Pay Gap).

Decline No. 3 — The cost of building a life
+1,200%

Essentials outran wages and inflation

Since 1980, college tuition is up about 1,200% and healthcare about 450%, while overall consumer prices rose about 236%. The dollar has lost over 96% of its 1913 purchasing power. The path to the middle class keeps getting more expensive.

Sources: Forbes Advisor; BLS via ihatecollege; Visual Capitalist.

There are real, honest disagreements between left and right — and Centrix respects them. But notice: none of the three declines above is a "left problem" or a "right problem." They worsened under both. That tells us the fight over who occupies the White House has distracted us from the fight that matters — actually empowering citizens to prosper. Our entire platform is built to reverse these three curves.

Where Centrix starts

Reversing those curves starts where you liveNational politics has had a century to fix the three declines above and has not. So Centrix begins somewhere a citizen can actually act: the economy within driving distance of their own front door.

The problem

The money doesn't stay

When the only place to buy something is a national chain or an online marketplace, the purchase still happens locally — but the margin leaves the moment the card clears. Dollars that could have paid a neighbour's wage become a line item in a distant quarterly report.

The waste

The skills sit unused

Meanwhile people who can build, grow, cook, fix, and make things work jobs well beneath their capability. The skill exists and the demand exists — nobody has connected the two, because nobody has looked at their own community as a market.

The opening

The gap is measurable

This is not a matter of opinion. You can count how many businesses of a given type your area has per resident, compare it to the national rate, and see precisely which needs are being served from outside — and therefore where a producer is genuinely needed.

Prosperity built close to home is resilient. It is the quiet lesson of communities like the Amish, who sustain durable local economies with low overhead, real skills, and reputations that take decades to build and seconds to lose. Keeping money circulating inside a community is not nostalgia — it is the most reliable way ordinary people have ever built wealth.

The economics underneath this
Every dollar has a direction. Independents recirculate roughly three times as much locally as chains — so a dollar that leaves is funding someone else's town, not vanishing.
Scale wins on price, loses elsewhere. You will not out-price a corporation. You do not have to, because price is only one thing people buy.
Overhead decides survival. Low fixed costs are not frugality — they are what lets you keep working through a bad quarter.
Reputation is capital. A competitor can match your prices in a day. Twenty years of doing what you said takes twenty years.

Our mission
To empower and educate every American to become a producer — self-reliant, prosperous, and free.

Centrix measures success by one question: Are ordinary citizens more capable of building a flourishing life this year than last? Not by which party won, not by who "owned" whom in the culture war — but by whether families can own homes, raise children, earn a fair return on honest work, and stand on their own feet.

We believe the American is not a consumer to be managed or a voter to be harvested, but a producer to be equipped. Government's job is not to run people's lives, and not to abandon them either — it is to hand them the tools, the skills, and the fair conditions to lift themselves.

Our philosophy

Keep the gold and the wisdom from each sideWe are not "the mushy middle." We are a deliberate synthesis: take what each tradition gets right, discard what it gets wrong, and aim the result squarely at empowering citizens.

Producers, not dependents

A dignified life comes from making things of value. We help citizens learn a trade, produce a good, and sell it — turning safety-net recipients into contributors, on their own timeline and with their dignity intact.

Localism over centralization

Prosperity built close to home is resilient. We revive local manufacturing and skilled production — drawing lessons from communities like the Amish who quietly sustain durable, self-reliant local economies.

A hand up, with an open door out

Keep a real safety net for those who need it — and pair every program with a clear, rewarding on-ramp back to productive work, so support is a bridge rather than a trap.

Home before empire

Fund the nation's own natural resources, technology, and people first. A restrained, non-interventionist foreign policy that invests in domestic strength rather than distant conflicts.

Family as the foundation

Stable families are the bedrock of a flourishing society. We make it dramatically easier to marry, have children, and own a home — with pro-family incentives drawn from successful policies used across many nations.

Freedom of conscience, firm on violence

Different communities may hold different values — and that is allowed. The one non-negotiable line: no ideology may justify violence against fellow citizens, ever.

The platform

What we'd actually doFor each issue: the wisdom the left brings, the wisdom the right brings, and the Centrix synthesis that turns both into empowerment.

1. Wages & the middle class

Left is right that…

Workers deserve a fair share of the wealth they create; decades of flat pay amid rising productivity is a real injustice.

Right is right that…

Heavy taxes and red tape choke the small businesses and startups that create good jobs in the first place.

The Centrix synthesis

Reward production directly: tax relief and 0% or low-interest capital for citizens and small firms that make and sell real goods, apprenticeship funding to raise skills, and profit-sharing incentives so pay tracks productivity again.

2. Local manufacturing & the producer economy

Left is right that…

Communities hollowed out by offshoring need active public investment, not just "let the market sort it out."

Right is right that…

Entrepreneurship, private ownership, and self-reliance — not permanent government jobs — build lasting prosperity.

The Centrix synthesis

A national program of open, systematically published guides — inspired by the durable self-sufficiency of Amish communities — teaching any citizen how to pick a resource or good, produce it, and sell it locally. Micro-grants and mentorship turn the guides into real workshops and shops. Then close the cost gap so a small producer can compete on price rather than on goodwill.

How you close the cost gap
  1. A local-content certification modelled on USDA Organic — a clear, auditable standard, such as no more than 5% of a product's inputs sourced from outside the country, with a stricter tier for genuinely regional production. Without a definition, every other measure here gets gamed.
  2. Tax relief on certified local goods, so the price a customer sees narrows against the imported equivalent instead of the producer absorbing the difference.
  3. Rebates on utilities and essential inputs — power, water, feed, fertiliser, packaging. Scale buyers already negotiate these discounts privately; this gives small producers something closer to parity.
  4. Local procurement preference for schools, hospitals, and municipal contracts wherever a certified supplier exists and the price is within a defined band of the market rate.
  5. Enforcement with real teeth — fraudulent certification treated as fraud, with penalties large enough to price it out, because a standard nobody polices is worse than no standard at all.

The honest tension: policing this cheaply means digitising it, and a database tracking who produced what edges toward surveillance nobody asked for. Price bands invite gaming in the other direction, too — a protected supplier with a guaranteed public buyer has every reason to charge the top of the band. Both problems are manageable, neither disappears, and any version of this that pretends otherwise is selling something. Why we think local production is worth the trouble →

3. Housing & starting a family

Left is right that…

Housing has become unaffordable and the market alone won't put a roof over working families.

Right is right that…

Ownership builds responsibility and wealth; strong, growing families are worth encouraging directly.

The Centrix synthesis

Pro-family, pro-ownership tools proven across many nations: tax relief that grows with each child, 0% or low-interest loans for first-time homebuilders and buyers, and a national goal of guaranteed access to affordable homeownership — paired with reform to actually build more homes.

4. The safety net & work

Left is right that…

No one in a wealthy nation should go without food, shelter, or medical care; a real safety net is a moral floor.

Right is right that…

Programs that punish work or trap people in dependency harm the very people they aim to help.

The Centrix synthesis

Keep the net strong — and redesign benefits so earning income phases support out gradually instead of yanking it away. Every recipient gets access to the producer guides and start-up capital, so the net becomes a launch pad. Then spend the net's own money locally, so it feeds families and builds producers at the same time.

Make the net buy local
  1. Certified local producers become eligible vendors for WIC, SNAP, school meals, and senior nutrition programs — so the household that most needs fresh food can buy it from a grower down the road, at program prices rather than farmers'-market prices.
  2. The subsidy does double duty. The same dollar feeds a family and pays a local producer, which is the multiplier working for the taxpayer instead of against them. Public money spent on food currently leaves most communities almost immediately.
  3. Means-test the pricing, not just the recipient. A guaranteed government buyer is an invitation to overcharge; eligibility should require prices within a defined band of prevailing market rates.

The honest tension: this only helps if the food is genuinely affordable at program prices, and small growers often cannot hit that number without the cost relief in Plank 2. The two measures work together or not at all — which is the argument for sequencing certification and input rebates first.

5. Healthcare & education costs

Left is right that…

Runaway medical and tuition bills bankrupt families and lock people out of opportunity.

Right is right that…

Bloated institutions and third-party subsidies inflate prices; competition and transparency discipline cost.

The Centrix synthesis

Attack the cost curve directly: price transparency, funding tied to student outcomes not administrative bloat, expanded trade and apprenticeship paths as real alternatives to costly degrees, and protection from catastrophic medical debt.

6. Foreign policy & national strength

Left is right that…

Endless wars cost lives and treasure and too often serve narrow interests over the public good.

Right is right that…

A nation must be genuinely strong, self-sufficient, and secure in its own resources and industry.

The Centrix synthesis

Principled non-intervention. Redirect funding from distant conflicts toward domestic natural resources, energy, and homegrown technological advancement — real strength built at home, not projected abroad.

7. Money in politics & the price of a seat

Left is right that…

When campaigns run on private money at the scale modern ones require, organised wealth sets the agenda before ordinary voters are consulted at all.

Right is right that…

Entrenched career politicians and unaccountable agencies escape the discipline voters are supposed to provide — and decisions belong closer to the people who live with them.

The Centrix synthesis

Neither party captured the government. The price of a seat did. When winning office requires raising sums only organised interests can supply, whoever wins owes the funder — which is why the three declines worsened under both parties. So fix the incentive, not the personnel.

Concrete measures — and where each one stalls
  1. Small-donor public matching. Multiply small contributions from constituents so a candidate can fund a serious campaign without large donors. Already working at city scale — New York's matching program and Seattle's democracy vouchers are the tested models, not hypotheticals.
  2. Ban individual stock trading by sitting members of Congress. This is live right now: the House passed a restricted version 232–198 on 22 July 2026, but it lets members keep and later sell holdings they already own, and it is widely expected to stall in the Senate. Senator Ossoff's stronger bill became the first such measure ever to clear a Senate committee. Centrix backs the version without the carve-out.
  3. Close the revolving door. A meaningful cooling-off period before members and senior staff may lobby, and a bar on moving directly between regulating an industry and being employed by it.
  4. Term limits for Congress — but only paired with permanent, well-funded nonpartisan legislative staff. See the tension below; on its own this measure backfires.
  5. The Article V route. Federal officeholders are unlikely to pass reforms that constrain federal officeholders. The Constitution offers a way around that: two-thirds of state legislatures (34) can call a convention to propose amendments, which three-quarters of the states (38) must then ratify — no Congressional permission required.

The honest tensions: the case for term limits is weaker than it feels. Research on state legislatures consistently finds term limits increase lobbyist influence and shift power toward governors and agencies, because when legislators rotate out quickly the people left holding the deepest institutional knowledge are the lobbyists and career staff who stay. That is why we pair the proposal with staff capacity rather than proposing it alone. The Article V route, meanwhile, has never once been used to call a convention, and there is no settled agreement on whether a convention's scope could be limited once it convened — a real risk that its advocates tend to wave away. And the deepest catch of all: nearly every measure here must be enacted by the people it constrains. That is precisely why Centrix does not ask you to wait for any of it. The platform is what we would argue for; the Production Assessment is what you can do on Monday regardless of whether a single line of it ever passes.

The controversial part — and we mean it

Different places, different values — sanctioned evenlyCentrix treats America's diversity of belief as a feature, not a bug. Where a community's values genuinely differ, we protect that difference — for everyone, on equal terms.

A left-leaning city that strongly supports abortion access and a traditional rural community with very different convictions are both recognized and protected under the same principle. We don't force one region's conscience onto another. Local majorities may live by their values; minorities keep their protected rights; and the same tolerance is extended in every direction — no side gets a privileged thumb on the scale.

Freedom of conscience Local self-determination Equal protection for all viewpoints No official ideology

The one absolute limit: no belief, community, or movement may support, threaten, or commit violence against fellow American citizens — at any time, for any reason. Tolerance of difference ends exactly where violence begins.

Common questions

Frequently askedStraight answers to the questions people ask first.

Is Centrix just "the middle" — splitting the difference on everything?
No. We don't average positions to look moderate. We take the strongest, most defensible idea from each side and combine them into policy aimed at one goal: empowering citizens to produce and prosper. Sometimes that lands left, sometimes right, sometimes somewhere new.
Are you left-wing or right-wing?
Neither, by design. We keep the left's commitment to a real safety net and fair wages, and the right's commitment to self-reliance, strong families, sound money, and local control — and we judge every policy by whether it helps ordinary Americans build a good life.
What is the "producer economy" and the local-manufacturing idea?
We want to make it normal and achievable for any citizen to pick a good or resource, learn to produce it, and sell it locally. Inspired by the durable self-sufficiency of communities like the Amish, we'd publish open, practical guides and back them with micro-grants and mentorship — turning consumers and benefit recipients into contributors.
Do you want to cut the safety net?
No. We keep it strong. We redesign benefits so that earning income phases support out gradually instead of yanking it away, and we pair every program with a real on-ramp to productive work. The net becomes a launch pad, not a trap.
How can you protect opposite values in different places at the same time?
We apply one rule evenly: local communities may live by their own values, individuals keep their protected rights, and the same tolerance extends in every direction. A progressive city and a traditional rural town are both recognized — no side gets a privileged thumb on the scale. The single absolute limit is that no belief may justify violence against fellow citizens.
What does an "isolationist" or non-interventionist foreign policy mean here?
Principled restraint abroad and investment at home. We'd redirect funding from distant conflicts toward domestic natural resources, energy, and homegrown technology — building genuine national strength rather than projecting it overseas.
Is this an official political party I can vote for?
Not yet — Centrix is an early-stage citizen movement and side project, written under the pen name Saint Moses the Black, not a ballot-registered party or a candidate campaign. Right now the goal is to test whether these ideas resonate: to get people reading the platform, debating it, and volunteering to build practical production manuals. Whether it grows into something more depends on people like you.
How can I actually help right now?
Three ways: join the list and community below so we can find each other; help write open "how to produce and sell a good locally" manuals for US (or other) citizens; and — maybe most important — talk about these ideas and their implications with people around you.
