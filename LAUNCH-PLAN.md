# Centrix — $2,000 Launch & Interest-Test Plan

**Goal:** Find out whether real people care about these ideas — *before* spending big. Get strangers to (1) read the platform and the data, (2) join the list and community, (3) volunteer to help write local-production manuals, and (4) talk about it and its implications.

This is a **test**, not a full campaign. Success is measured in signal, not vanity metrics.

---

## What "interest" looks like (your success metrics)

Pick a few numbers up front so you can judge honestly at the end of ~30 days:

| Metric | Weak signal | Promising | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unique visitors to the site | < 1,000 | 3,000–8,000 | 15,000+ |
| Email subscribers | < 100 | 300–800 | 1,500+ |
| Discord members | < 50 | 150–400 | 800+ |
| People volunteering to write a manual | 0–3 | 8–20 | 40+ |
| Organic shares / real discussion threads | a handful | dozens | it spreads without you paying |

The single most important one is **email subscribers who came from strangers** (not friends and family). That's the closest thing to proof that the idea — not your personal network — has pull.

## Step 1 — Build the free foundation first ($0, do this before spending a cent)

None of the paid stuff matters if there's nowhere for people to land and stay.

- **Domain** — buy something like `centrix.us` or `centrixmovement.org` (~$10–20/yr). This is the one near-essential paid item.
- **Hosting** — **Cloudflare Pages** (recommended — see the hosting section in the README; free, unlimited bandwidth, global CDN, so it won't fall over if a post goes viral).
- **Email list** — **Substack** (free). It's your newsletter, your archive of ideas, *and* your email capture, all in one. Publish the founding essay here on day one.
- **Community** — a free **Discord** server with a few channels: `#welcome`, `#discuss`, `#the-data`, `#manuals-project`, `#your-town`.
- **Form backend** — connect the site's sign-up form to **Formspree** (free tier) or embed Substack's subscribe box directly.
- **Analytics** — add **Plausible** (paid but cheap and privacy-friendly) or **Google Analytics / Cloudflare Web Analytics** (free) so you can actually see what's working.
- **One social home base** — an **X/Twitter** account and a **subreddit** (or post into existing ones — see organic strategy).

Time, not money, is the cost here. Budget a weekend.

## Step 2 — Spend the $2,000 (suggested allocation)

Numbers are planning estimates; ad prices vary a lot by audience and season. Treat this as a starting split you adjust weekly based on what performs.

| Line item | Budget | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Domain + basic tools (Plausible, etc.) | **$80** | The permanent home and measurement. |
| Reddit ads | **$500** | Cheap, and Reddit is where people actually *debate ideas* — perfect for "discuss the implications." Target relevant subreddits. |
| X / Twitter ads (promoted posts) | **$450** | Amplify your best threads to drive traffic + follows. |
| Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads | **$500** | Broadest reach and best email-signup conversion — **but note the compliance step below.** |
| Short-form video / creative | **$250** | One or two simple explainer clips (the three declines) for TikTok/Reels/Shorts. You can DIY this to save money and reallocate to ads. |
| Newsletter / creator cross-promotion | **$150** | A paid shout-out in a like-minded independent newsletter often outperforms cold ads. |
| Reserve / double-down fund | **$70** | Hold back a little to pour into whatever channel is clearly winning by week 3. |

**Rule of thumb:** start each paid channel with a small test ($50–100), kill the losers fast, and move money to the one channel that's driving the cheapest *email signups* (not clicks, not likes — signups).

## Step 3 — Organic strategy (free, and often the real winner)

Paid ads buy a spike; organic builds the movement. Do both.

- **X/Twitter:** post the "three declines" as a punchy thread with the charts from `data.html`. The hook is bipartisan frustration — *"this got worse under both parties."* That framing travels.
- **Reddit:** don't just run ads — participate. Share the data page in discussion-friendly communities (economics, personal finance, housing, politics-adjacent) where genuine debate is welcome. Read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first; lead with the *ideas*, not a sales pitch.
- **Short video:** a 45-second "3 numbers that should scare both parties" clip is ideal for Reels/TikTok/Shorts.
- **The founding essay on Substack:** the long-form "why I started this" from Michael. This is what serious people share.
- **Ask for the specific action:** every post ends with one clear ask — *read the data, join the discussion, or help write a manual.*

## Step 4 — A simple 4-week timeline

- **Week 0 (setup):** domain, hosting, Substack, Discord, analytics, form connected. Publish founding essay. Complete ad-platform identity verification (see below — start this *first*, it takes days).
- **Week 1:** organic launch. Post the thread + video, seed the subreddit conversation, invite your personal network to be the first 50 members so newcomers don't arrive to an empty room.
- **Week 2:** turn on paid ads with small test budgets across all channels. Watch cost-per-signup.
- **Week 3:** cut the weak channels, pour the reserve into the winner. Start the manuals project with early volunteers — a public, visible first manual is powerful proof.
- **Week 4:** measure against the table above, write a candid recap for subscribers, and decide: double down, pivot the message, or wind down.

## Important: political-ad compliance (read before you spend)

Because Centrix is a political / "social issue" movement, the big ad platforms treat your ads specially — this is a real step, not a formality:

- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram)** requires you to **verify your identity** (government photo ID + a confirmation code mailed to your address, typically **5–10 business days**) and to run a **"Paid for by" disclaimer** on political/social-issue ads. Ads without it get rejected. *Start this the very first day so it's ready before week 2.*
- **Google/YouTube** has a similar advertiser identity-verification process for election/political content.
- **Reddit and X** have their own political-ads policies; check current rules before booking.

Source: [Meta — Get Authorized to Run Ads About Social Issues, Elections or Politics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/208949576550051).

## Important: money & legal (not legal advice)

You chose **engagement-first, no donations yet** — which is the lower-risk path, and a good call for a test. A couple of things to keep in mind if that changes:

- The moment you start **raising or spending money to influence federal elections**, U.S. campaign-finance rules can kick in (the federal threshold for "political committee" status is low — around $1,000). State rules vary and can be stricter.
- If you later want to **collect donations**, talk to a campaign-finance / nonprofit attorney *first*, and consider a proper structure (LLC, nonprofit, or fiscal sponsor) before any money changes hands.
- This document is practical guidance, **not legal advice.** For anything involving raising money or election activity, get a professional opinion.

## A note on integrity

Test honestly. Don't buy fake followers or engagement — it poisons the very signal you're paying to measure. Be upfront that Centrix is brand-new and a small side project written under a pen name (Saint Moses the Black); let the ideas stand on their own. That honesty is the whole pitch; live it from day one.
