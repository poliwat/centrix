Production Assessment Methodology

How Centrix identifies what your local economy is missing — and what the method cannot tell you.

The idea being measured

When a community has fewer businesses in a category than the country does on a per-person basis, residents are usually still buying that thing. They are just buying it somewhere else — a chain a few towns over, or online. The purchase still happens; the margin leaves. Economists call this economic leakage.

Leakage is an opportunity. It marks demand that already exists locally and is currently being satisfied by someone who does not live there. That is a very different starting position from inventing demand from scratch.

The calculation

For each business category we compare local establishment density against the national rate:

local density = local establishments ÷ local population × 10,000
national density = national establishments ÷ national population × 10,000

gap ratio = local density ÷ national density

A ratio below 1 means the area has proportionally fewer such businesses than the nation.

Gap ratioClassificationInterpretation
below 0.6Under-servedNotably fewer than the national norm — likely leakage
0.6 – 1.4Near national normRoughly typical provision
above 1.4SaturatedMore than typical — entering means taking share

We also report implied missing establishments: how many more businesses the area would contain if it matched the national rate. This converts an abstract ratio into a count.

implied missing = round(national density × local population ÷ 10,000) − local establishments

Worked example

A ZIP with 25,000 residents has 3 food-and-beverage retailers. Local density is 1.20 per 10,000. If the national density is 1.93 per 10,000, the gap ratio is 0.62 — under-served — and matching the national rate would mean about 2 more such businesses.

Chain versus independent

Where OpenStreetMap coverage allows, we also count how many nearby businesses carry a recognized brand or corporate operator tag. A high chain share alongside an under-served category is the clearest signal that local demand is being met by firms headquartered elsewhere.

Search radius scales with community type: roughly 3 km urban, 6 km suburban, 12 km rural, 20 km remote — because a rural household's realistic shopping radius is not a city block.

What this method cannot tell you

These limitations are real and we would rather state them than have you discover them after spending money.

Solo operators are invisible

County Business Patterns counts only establishments with paid employees. A one-person bakery, a weekend furniture maker, a self-employed contractor — none appear. A category showing zero may already have working competitors. This is the single most important caveat.

People cross ZIP lines

A residential ZIP next to a commercial one will look under-served while its residents shop five minutes away. Under-served means worth verifying, not unmet.

The data lags

CBP figures are from 2022. Businesses have opened and closed since. Treat the analysis as a starting hypothesis about your area, not a current census of it.

Per-capita demand is an assumption

The method assumes demand scales with resident population. That breaks in tourist towns, college towns, agricultural areas, and commuter suburbs, where the customer base is not the same as the population.

Small numbers are noisy

In a low-population ZIP, one establishment either way swings the ratio hard. Treat categories with very low counts as directional only.

How to verify a gap before you spend

  1. Drive or walk the area and count what actually exists — including the solo operators Census misses.
  2. Search Facebook Marketplace and local groups; informal businesses live there, not in official data.
  3. Ask ten people where they currently buy it and what they dislike about that. If they name somewhere out of town, the leakage is real.
  4. Check whether the gap has an obvious cause — zoning, licensing, a dominant supplier, or a business that already tried and failed.
  5. Presell before you build. One paying customer beats any statistic on this page.

Reproducing this

Every figure comes from public APIs; nothing is proprietary. The calculation lives in _worker.js in the Centrix repository, and the diagnostic endpoint /api/probe?zip=YOURZIP returns the raw upstream responses for any ZIP so you can check our arithmetic against the source data yourself.

If you find an error in this methodology, that is worth more to us than a compliment. The whole point of publishing it is to be checkable.
